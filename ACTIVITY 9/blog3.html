Faster turns, fewer regrips

The Best Finger Tricks for Speedcubing

Finger tricks are the specific ways you turn the cube using your fingers, wrists, and thumbs. Good finger tricks let you perform moves quickly and smoothly without regripping, which is one of the most important parts of fast turning.

Why Finger Tricks Matter

  • They let you turn faster with less effort.
  • They reduce regrips, which waste time.
  • They make your turning smoother, which also helps lookahead.

Start with a Good Grip

Most speedcubers hold the cube with the left and right hands on the sides, with the thumbs resting on the front or bottom. Keep your grip relaxed. A tense grip slows your turns and tires your hands. Your index fingers should be free to flick the top layer.

The Essential Finger Tricks

Index finger flick (U and U')

The most common trick. Use your right index finger to flick the top layer for U, and your left index finger for U'. Keep the motion short and light.

Double flick (U2)

For a U2 turn, flick twice in a row with the same index finger. It is much faster than turning with the whole hand.

Wrist flick (R and R')

For R-face turns, use a small wrist motion rather than moving your whole arm. This is the foundation of fast R moves in many common algorithms.

Ring finger pull (D and D')

Use the ring finger to pull the bottom layer for D turns. It is quicker than regripping to turn D.

Thumb moves (F and F')

The right thumb can push the front layer for F turns, again avoiding a regrip.

Push and pull for L moves

For L turns, use the left hand fingers or wrist in the same way as the right hand, so both hands stay equally fast.

How to Practice Finger Tricks

  • Isolate each move. Repeat each one 20 to 30 times slowly before speeding up.
  • Practice common algorithms such as the Sexy Move (R U R' U') as a drill, because it contains many of the tricks above.
  • Focus on smoothness first and speed second. Speed comes naturally from clean motion.
  • Record yourself and check whether you are regripping unnecessarily.

Common Mistakes

  • Gripping too tightly, which slows down turning.
  • Over-rotating the wrist or whole arm when a small finger flick would do.
  • Rushing before the motion is clean, which builds bad habits.
  • Neglecting the weaker hand, which then holds back your algorithms.

Choose Algorithms That Fit Your Hands

Many algorithms have more than one version. Pick the version that feels the most comfortable and uses the best finger tricks for you, not just the one that looks shortest on paper.

Pro tip: If a move feels awkward, do not just push through it. Try a different grip or alternate algorithm. Comfortable moves become fast moves.
